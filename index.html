Your sound stays on your Mac

SoundGate has no account, no analytics and no servers. All audio processing happens on your Mac in real time — nothing is recorded, stored or uploaded. Its only network request is a daily check for new versions on GitHub, which you can turn off. If you turn on Sync, your settings go to a cloud folder you choose — your own iCloud Drive, Google Drive or OneDrive — never to SoundGate's developer.

FAQ

Questions

Is SoundGate free?

Yes.

Which Macs does it run on?

Macs with Apple silicon running macOS 14.2 (Sonoma) or later. Control Center buttons need macOS 26 or later.

Does it work with my headphones?

Any headphones get spatial audio's speakers-in-front effect. Head tracking needs AirPods (3rd generation or later), AirPods Pro, AirPods Max or supported Beats. If your AirPods already use Apple's “Spatialize Stereo”, turn that off in Control Center so the sound isn't processed twice.

My monitor's volume keys don't do anything on a Mac. Will this fix it?

Usually, yes. Most monitors let SoundGate change their speaker volume directly over the display cable (DDC). If yours doesn't, SoundGate tells you so in the app.

Why does it need System Audio Recording?

That's the macOS permission that lets an app process other apps' sound — needed for per-app volume, the EQ and spatial audio. SoundGate only processes the sound live; nothing is recorded.

How does SoundGate stay up to date?

It checks this site's releases about once a day and lets you know when a new version is out — install it with one click from the menu bar. Turn on Install updates automatically to have SoundGate download and install updates itself (macOS asks for your password to update an app in Applications). Every update is checked to be signed by SoundGate's developer and notarized by Apple before it's installed. You can switch off update checks entirely under Devices & Settings → About.

Can I use the same settings on another Mac?

Yes. Turn on Devices & Settings → Sync on each Mac and pick the same cloud folder — iCloud Drive, Google Drive, OneDrive, Dropbox or any folder your cloud app syncs. Your saved EQ presets, hearing test results and each device's sound stay in step; turn on Also sync all settings to include priorities, per-app volumes and appearance. The most recent change wins, and a Mac that joins later keeps its own saved presets.

Your settings are saved as SoundGate › SoundGate Settings.plist inside the cloud folder you pick — for example iCloud Drive › SoundGate › SoundGate Settings.plist. SoundGate shows the exact location under Sync, with a Show in Finder button.

How do I uninstall it?

Quit SoundGate from its menu, then drag SoundGate from Applications to the Trash. To also remove its settings, run defaults delete com.markhydar.SoundGate in Terminal.

Troubleshooting

Something not working?

Most problems come down to a macOS permission. Here's how to fix the common ones.

The volume keys don't change my monitor's volume

SoundGate needs Accessibility permission to read the volume keys — if it's missing, the menu bar panel shows a warning with a Grant… button.

  1. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility.
  2. If SoundGate is already in the list, select it and click − to remove it. (macOS ties the permission to one exact copy of the app, so an entry left over from an older or reinstalled copy can look switched on but no longer apply.)
  3. Click +, choose Applications → SoundGate, and switch it on.

No restart needed — the keys start working within a couple of seconds and the warning disappears.

The keys work, but the monitor's volume doesn't change

SoundGate changes the volume over the display cable (DDC). Make sure DDC/CI is turned on in the monitor's on-screen menu, and that its speakers aren't muted there. Some docks, hubs and adapters don't pass DDC through — try connecting the monitor directly with a USB‑C or DisplayPort cable. If your monitor doesn't support it, SoundGate says so in the app.

Sound doesn't come out of my monitor

The menu bar panel shows where sound is playing (“Playing through …”). In Devices & Settings → Output Priority, move your monitor to the top and turn on Automatically use the highest-priority output. Also check the monitor's own menu — some monitors have a separate audio-source or speaker setting.

Per-app volume, the EQ or spatial audio have no effect

These need System Audio Recording permission. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security → Screen & System Audio Recording, and under System Audio Recording Only switch on SoundGate. If it's already on but nothing changes, remove it with −, add it again with +, then quit and reopen SoundGate.

Head tracking doesn't follow my head

Head tracking needs supported AirPods or Beats, worn and connected, and the Motion & Fitness permission (System Settings → Privacy & Security → Motion & Fitness). Turn off Apple's “Spatialize Stereo” for your AirPods in Control Center so the sound isn't processed twice, and use Re-centre to reset which way is forward.

I can't find SoundGate in the menu bar

On Macs with a notch, menu bar icons can be hidden when there are too many — quit an app or two to make room. On macOS 26 and later, check that SoundGate is allowed under System Settings → Menu Bar. You can always open SoundGate from Applications to bring up its window, and switch Show SoundGate in to include the Dock.

Settings don't sync to my other Mac

Both Macs need SoundGate 1.0.3 or later with Sync on and the same cloud folder chosen, and the cloud app (iCloud Drive, Google Drive, OneDrive…) has to be running and signed in to the same account on both. Changes usually arrive within a minute — click Sync Now to check straight away. If macOS asks whether SoundGate may access files in your cloud folder, click Allow.

An update didn't install

Download the latest version from the button at the top of this page and run the installer — your settings are kept. If SoundGate says an update “isn't signed by SoundGate's developer”, it refused to install a download it couldn't verify; downloading it fresh from here fixes that.

Still stuck? Report a problem — say which Mac, macOS version and monitor or headphones you're using.
